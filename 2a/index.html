Basic rung · Tool 2.a

The honest picture, from your reviews

Your last 50 reviews already say what guests wish they had known. AI sorts them and drafts a short "What to expect, honestly" section and a reply to each unanswered review. You check every quote and approve every word. A person posts every reply.

  • Time about 2 hours
  • Cost free tier
  • Skill no code
  • Measure reply rate · Number 4
  • Feeds 2.b, 3.a

The pain it fixes

For the traveler

  • 43% of US travelers say second-guessing whether they chose right is a frustrating part of planning. 52% feel anxious about booking the wrong option.
  • 41% put content from travelers who have been there in their top three sources of confidence.
  • Accor tested hotel pages with and without reviews. Genuine reviews, even bad ones, built more confidence than curated brand copy. No lift figure was published.

For your business

Doubt shows up twice: as inquiries that never book, and as changes and cancellations after booking. (Our read, not a survey finding.)

A plain list of what to expect, flaws included, answers that doubt before it costs you. Replies show the next traveler that a person reads and answers.

The cleaned reviews and the approved section also become the knowledge for 2.b, the grounded answer assistant.

Source: Skift Research + McKinsey, Winning Hearts in an Age of Infinite Travel Choices, Sept 2026 · survey of 1,015 US travelers, data as of June 2026. US travelers, not Alaska visitors.

How to use it

  1. Collect your last 50 reviews from Google, Tripadvisor, Viator or your booking platform. Copy and paste them, or load a CSV. About 30 minutes.
  2. Clean them here. The page removes emails, phone numbers, links and long numbers, and flags likely names for you to check.
  3. Look at the counts. They are keyword hints, not the analysis.
  4. Copy the Step 1 prompt into Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini. A free tier works.
  5. Check the answer here. Fix any quote that is not in your reviews.
  6. Approve the "What to expect, honestly" section. The owner edits and approves it, then you publish it on your website and on booking pages that allow it.
  7. Reply. Copy the Step 2 prompt for up to 5 unanswered reviews, check the drafts, and have a person edit and post each one. Log it.
  8. Count your reply rate now and every month. Refresh the section when the review mix changes.

Times are estimates for a small operator with reviews on two or three platforms.

What the cleaner does, and does not do

It replaces emails, phone numbers, web links, street addresses and numbers of 8 or more digits (booking or card numbers) with a label such as [PHONE REMOVED], in the prompt only. It flags capitalized words in mid-sentence and words after Captain, guide or deckhand as possible names. It does not catch everything: a name at the start of a sentence, a nickname or a home town can slip through. Read the reviews once before you copy the prompt.

If you load a CSV, columns that look like names, emails, phones, links or booking numbers are left out, and the page tells you which.

      What it checks

How you will know it worked

    
    
      
  • Review reply rate: replies posted, divided by reviews received. Count it monthly from your reply log.
    • 
      
  • Inquiry-to-booking rate: bookings from inquiries (email, phone, web form), divided by inquiries. Monthly.
    • 
      
  • In-window changes and cancellations (Number 4): bookings changed or canceled inside the free-cancellation window, divided by bookings. Monthly in season; count flexible and non-refundable bookings separately. Judge it only after a season of data.
    • 
    

    
Count a baseline before you publish the section, or there is nothing to compare. These numbers also move with weather, prices and ships in port, so do not credit a change to the section alone.

Before you use it

    
AI drafts. You approve. Every answer is marked DRAFT - PENDING OWNER REVIEW until the owner has read it.

    
    
      
  • Legal mandate No fake reviews. The FTC rule on consumer reviews and testimonials, 16 CFR Part 465 (in effect since October 2024), bans writing, buying or posting fake reviews, including AI-generated ones, and reviews from people who did not have the experience (465.2). It also bans rewards tied to a positive or negative review (465.4), requires staff and family reviews to disclose the connection (465.5), and bans threats to get a review removed or hiding bad reviews on your own site while implying you show all or most of them (465.7).
    • 
      
  • Recommendation Never write reviews, and never have AI write them, for your business or anyone else's. This tool reads reviews and drafts replies; it never writes a review.
    • 
      
  • Contractual Before you copy review text onto your website or ads, check each platform's terms of service (Google, Tripadvisor, Viator). The "What to expect" section is written in your own words, not copied from reviews.
    • 
      
  • Recommendation Remove guest names and contact details before you paste anything into an AI tool, and check your AI tool's data settings. This page does a first pass only.
    • 
      
  • Recommendation Anything about sea conditions, weather, seasickness or safety in the section is owned by your captain or another qualified person. AI drafts it; that person approves it. No medical advice.
    • 
      
  • Policy choice The owner approves the section before it is published, and a person posts every reply.
    • 
      
  • Recommendation Keep the section current. Re-run Step 1 monthly in season, or when the review mix changes.
    • 
    

    
The quote checker proves only that the words exist in a review. Read each review yourself.
