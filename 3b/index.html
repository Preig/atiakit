ATIA AI Take-Home Kit › Pain 3 · Coordinating the trip

Intermediate rung · Tool 3.b

A partner circle with shared facts

Three to five businesses that complete each other's trips keep one shared fact sheet. A no-code automation then drafts a trip kit for every booking a month before arrival, with partner options and referral codes. Each kit waits as a Gmail draft until one person checks it and sends it. The codes show what the circle is worth.

  • Time about a week (estimate)
  • Cost automation plan roughly $20 to $30 a month, plus cents per kit for the AI step (estimate)
  • Skill no code: Zapier or Make
  • Measure cross-referrals by code · attach rate
  • Needs 3.a, and 1.a from every partner
  • Feeds 3.c

The pain it fixes

For the traveler

  • 39% of US travelers name coordinating across different parts of the trip as a frustrating moment in planning.
  • 78% keep planning after they book, and 68% research restaurants and activities.
  • In a diary study of 26 travelers, the handoffs between platforms were the most frequent source of negative feelings. Treat that one as directional.

For your business

Alaska trips are multi-business by default: a cruise, a meal, a kennel visit, a drive. Every handoff is a place where the guest starts over. (Our read; nobody has measured this for Alaska.)

Many operators already send guests to each other. What is usually missing is one set of facts everyone agrees on, and a count of who sent whom.

One sheet, kept true by named owners, lets the AI draft a kit that is right about every partner. The codes turn referrals into a number you can check each month.

Sources: Skift Research + McKinsey, Winning Hearts in an Age of Infinite Travel Choices, Sept 2026, n = 1,015 US travelers (survey data as of June 2026). Diary finding: Skift Research Booking Journey Diary Study 2026, 26 travelers and 211 sessions, July 2026. US travelers, not Alaska visitors.

How it works

  1. Booking inA row goes into your queue sheet: dates, party, product, and send_date = arrival minus 30 days.
  2. Every morningThe automation picks rows that are queued and due: send_date today or earlier.
  3. AI draftsFrom the shared fact sheet and the booking fields. Nothing else reaches the AI step.
  4. Gmail draftThe kit is saved as a draft. Nothing is sent.
  5. A person approvesOne person checks the draft, sends it, and records it in the sheet.
  6. Monthly countPartners count the codes and agree the numbers.

Why a sheet, and not a "wait until 30 days before arrival" step? A delay step holds each booking inside the automation for weeks or months. A booking made far ahead can need a longer wait than the product allows (Make's sleep step waits minutes, not weeks; check Zapier's current delay limit). A held booking also misses date changes and cancellations, and you cannot see what is waiting. A queue sheet with a send_date column is visible, editable and handles all of this, including late bookings.

Your week

  1. Day 1: agree the circle with your partners (Step 1). Pick the businesses, the codes and who approves kits. About 2 hours together.
  2. Day 2: fill the shared fact sheet (Step 2). Each partner fills or approves its own block. About 30 minutes per partner.
  3. Day 3: set up the queue sheet and paste in the five fictional test bookings (Step 3). About 1 hour.
  4. Day 4: dry-run the AI step by hand and check the draft (Step 4), then build the automation in Zapier or Make (Step 5). 3 to 5 hours.
  5. Day 5: run the five fictional bookings end to end, drafts only (Step 6). Fix what you find, then connect real bookings.
  6. Every month: reconcile the codes (Step 7), note the attach rate, and re-check any block older than 60 days.

Times are estimates for a small operator who already has a 1.a fact sheet and has used 3.a by hand.

Step 1. Agree the circle

Agree the rules before you build anything. The referral codes are the measurement, so agree them first. The agreement template is a starting checklist, not legal advice. Each line says what kind of obligation it is.

  • Recommendation Three to five businesses that complete each other's trips: a lodge, a cruise, a kayak outfitter, a restaurant. Not two businesses selling the same hour to the same guest.
  • Policy choice Approvals. One named person at the business that sends the kit approves each kit before it goes out. Each partner approves the wording of its own block before it is used.
  • Policy choice Fact owners. Each block has one named owner who re-checks it at least every 60 days and before the season opens, and tells the circle about a change within two business days.
  • Contractual Referral codes. One unique code for each pair of businesses (the one that sends the guest, the one that honors the code), what the guest gets with it, and any referral fee, written into your signed partner agreement. Check each booking platform's terms on promo codes.
  • Policy choice Monthly reconciliation. Each business sends its code counts by the 5th of the month, and the circle settles differences within a week (Step 7).
  • Policy choice Data. The AI step receives dates, party size and ages, and the product booked. Nothing else: no names, emails, phone numbers, payment details or medical notes.
  • Contractual Tools. Check the terms and data processing agreements of Zapier or Make, your AI provider, Google Workspace and your booking platform before you connect them.
  • Legal mandate Guide licensing. A partner that guides big game hunting needs its license under AS 08.54; a sport fishing guide and business register with ADF&G each year. That license stays with that partner. It is not delegable to the circle, the coordinator or an AI tool.

Download the agreement checklist (.md)

A .md file is plain text. Open it in Notepad, TextEdit, Word or Google Docs, fill the brackets, and have every partner review it. Ask your attorney before you sign anything that involves money.

Step 2. Build the shared fact sheet

One block per business, including yours. Each partner fills its own block, or approves what you typed. The sheet goes into the AI step for every booking, so use published, approved facts only.

Business facts only. No guest names, emails, phone numbers or booking details in this sheet. Everything stays in this browser.

A block is flagged when a required field is empty or it was last checked more than 60 days ago. Empty fields become [FILL] in the AI step, so the AI asks instead of guessing. Import reads shared-facts-template.csv, a CSV saved here, or the partner list saved from 3.a.

The fictional example has July only. For any other month the AI step writes [FILL: month note] instead of describing weather.

    Step 5. Build the automation in Zapier or Make

No ready-made template files are shipped, because they are tied to each account's connections.

    Step 6. Test with five fictional bookings, then approve every kit
    
    
    Run the whole chain on the five fictional rows before any real booking touches it.
    
    
      
      
    1. Copy the five TEST rows from kit-queue-template.csv into your queue tab. Change send_date on TEST-001 to TEST-004 to today's date. Leave TEST-005 as canceled.
      2. 
      
    2. Put the fact sheet text and month notes in the facts tab (the fictional ones are fine for the test).
      3. 
      
    3. Run the daily automation once by hand. Zapier: test it step by step, or turn it on and run it. Make: Run once.
      4. 
      
    4. Expect four Gmail drafts and nothing in Sent. TEST-005 is untouched. Four rows now say drafted, with a date and a draft id.
      5. 
      
    5. Run it again. Expect no new drafts: those rows are no longer queued.
      6. 
      
    6. Open each draft. Paste it into Check the draft and go through the approval checklist below. Expect TEST-001 to leave out the kayak (minimum age 12), and the June and August kits to ask for a month note.
      7. 
      
    7. Delete the four test drafts and the TEST rows. Only then point the booking trigger at real bookings. Keep drafts only.
      8. 
    
    
    
    The approval checklist: one person, every kit
    
    
    
      
    Kit approval check
    
      
    KIT APPROVAL CHECK: one person, every kit
[ ] Booked product, date and time match the booking system.
[ ] Every partner option fits the party's ages (minimum age in the fact sheet).
[ ] No partner option overlaps the booking once drive time is added.
[ ] Every fact matches the shared fact sheet. Nothing added, nothing rounded.
[ ] Referral codes are exactly as in the sheet.
[ ] If you earn referral fees from these partners, the kit says so.
[ ] No promises about availability, prices, weather or wildlife.
[ ] Safety wording (weather, water, glacier, wildlife) is each business's own wording.
[ ] Every [FILL] is answered or its line deleted. QUESTIONS FOR THE OWNER is answered, then removed.
[ ] The guest's address goes in the To field, from the booking system. No other guest details in the body.
[ ] Both DRAFT - PENDING OWNER REVIEW lines deleted, last.
Then send it. In the queue sheet: your initials in approved_by, the date in sent_date, status sent.

    How you will know it worked
    
    
      
      
    • Cross-referrals by code. Per code, per month: the agreed count from Step 7. Write down a baseline first: the month before the circle starts, including any informal count partners kept.
      • 
      
    • Attach rate per booking. Partner bookings with a circle code, divided by kits sent. Count it monthly in season. The helper above works it out from agreed codes.
      • 
      
    • Also watch: minutes per approval (your cost) and repeat questions per booking (Number 3). Fewer "what should we do after the cruise?" emails is the kit doing its job.
      • 
    
    
    
    After one season, decide whether to keep, revise or stop, using the "Decide whether to keep a rung" prompt in the printable prompt kit. Do not turn referrals into revenue claims without the partners' agreed counts.

    Before you use it
    
    
    AI drafts. A person approves. Every kit is a Gmail draft marked DRAFT - PENDING OWNER REVIEW until one named person checks it and sends it. An AI that checks its own work is not verification.
    
    
      
      
    • Policy choice Drafts only. Nothing in these recipes sends email. One named person approves each kit.
      • 
      
    • Policy choice Safety calls stay with people. Weather go/no-go, water, glacier, wildlife, aviation and first aid are decided by each business's qualified person. The kit repeats their conditions; it never makes the call.
      • 
      
    • Legal mandate Guide licensing. Big game guiding is licensed under AS 08.54, and sport fishing guides and businesses register with ADF&G each year. Each licensed partner keeps its own license current. Not delegable.
      • 
      
    • Policy choice Guest data. The AI step gets dates, party size and ages, and product only. Never paste guest names, emails, phone numbers, card numbers, IDs or medical details into this page or the AI step.
      • 
      
    • Contractual Data terms. Check the terms of Zapier or Make, your AI provider, Google Workspace and your booking platform before you connect them. Some booking platforms limit what you may send to other tools.
      • 
      
    • Recommendation Say who your partners are. The prompt calls them "our partner businesses". If you earn referral fees, say so, and ask your attorney whether the FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255) apply.
      • 
      
    • Recommendation Keep the kit about the guest's own trip. Partner offers can make an email commercial under the CAN-SPAM Act. Ask your attorney, and give guests an easy way to opt out of partner suggestions.
      • 
      
    • Recommendation Share published facts only. Do not agree on prices with a business that competes with you for the same guests.
      • 
    
    
    
    Bay Light Day Cruises, Mile 12 Kennel and Harbor Kayak Co. are fictional teaching businesses. Your entries stay in this browser only (local storage), and nothing goes into a URL.
