Post-booking retention engine

AI drafts. You approve. The pilot writes every draft as DRAFT - PENDING OWNER REVIEW into a spreadsheet, and a person sends approved messages from the booking platform. The examples use Bay Light Day Cruises, Seward, a fictional teaching business. What you type on this page stays in this browser.

The pain it fixes

For the traveler

78%keep planning after they book. Only 18% stop.

4 in 10have changed a booked stay after seeing a better option, and 41% watch the price of a booking they already hold.

The booking never feels final. Doubt keeps working after the payment.

For your business (my read)

Doubt shows up after the booking as changes and cancellations inside the free-cancellation window. A seat freed two days out is hard to resell. Chasing every flexible booking with discounts costs more than it saves.

The fix is not a price cut. It is a booking that feels safe to keep.

What would raise confidence

Asked what would make them more comfortable with AI-assisted booking, travelers chose:

  • The ability to review and change before confirming52%
  • A track record30%
  • A human who can step in20%

So: confidence first, discount last. The same three wishes make a good post-booking message (my read). Message A, the confidence note, says what to expect and how to review or change the booking, gives one checkable proof point, and names a person who can step in. A discount comes last, if at all, and only on terms the owner writes.

Is it for you?

Built for: operators with about 1,500 or more bookings a season on a booking platform with an API (FareHarbor, Peek, Checkfront, Cloudbeds or similar), plus a developer or vendor. A multi-day tour company or a 20-room lodge fits. You also need 2.a (the honest picture) and 2.b (approved facts) done first, because message A is built from them.

Smaller? The post-booking calendar from the session (five messages, one job each) gets most of the benefit with no code. The demo below still teaches the method.

Post-booking message rules

Write these down before the first draft. The reference code builds them in.

  • Policy choice Nothing sends without a person. Drafts land in an approval queue as PENDING. The reference code has no sending function at all, and a test fails if anyone adds one.
  • Policy choice No competitor price matching. Do not match rates and do not chase every cancellable booking. Pick the most exposed bookings by a rule you can read.
  • Recommendation Confidence first, discount last. Every treatment booking gets message A. At most one follow-up: B (one add-on) or C (lock-in offer). C exists only if the owner writes its terms.
  • Recommendation Approved facts only. Drafts use your fact sheet, your approved add-on list and your written terms. Anything missing stays as [FILL] for a person to supply. Asking the AI to check its own draft is not verification.
  • Policy choice Contractual No guest personal data in the pilot. Only booking id, dates, rate type, channel, party size and add-ons. Names, emails, phones and payment data stay in the platform. Check your platform's API terms and the model provider's data terms.
  • Contractual Promotions to OTA guests. B and C are promotional, so by default they go only to direct bookings. Check your OTA supplier agreement (for example Viator's) and any hotel-desk agreement before contacting their guests with offers.
  • Legal mandate Commercial email and texts. Promotional email needs an honest subject line, your postal address, a working opt-out and, unless the guest agreed to receive it, a clear note that it is an ad. Opt-outs must be honored (CAN-SPAM Act, 15 U.S.C. 7701 to 7713; 16 CFR Part 316). Marketing texts sent with automated tools generally need prior express written consent (TCPA, 47 U.S.C. 227; 47 CFR 64.1200). Ask your platform how its messaging handles this. This is not legal advice.
  • Legal mandate Lodging prices. A lodge offer that shows a price must show the total price, including mandatory fees (16 CFR Part 464, in effect since May 12, 2025). The rule covers short-term lodging, not tours.
  • Policy choice Safety wording has an owner. Weather plan B, sea state, glacier and wildlife lines are drafted by AI and owned by your captain or operations lead. No promised sightings.
  • Recommendation Holdout guests lose nothing. They still get every normal confirmation, safety and weather message. Only the pilot's extra messages are held back.
  • Recommendation Stop rule. Pause the pilot if complaints or opt-outs rise, and decide before you start who makes that call.

How it works

  1. Pull. Each night, export open bookings. The adapter keeps an allow-list of columns, drops everything else and logs the dropped column names, never their values.
  2. Score. A rule set you can read and change. Higher score means more likely to change or cancel inside the free-cancellation window. The top 10% are eligible.
  3. Assign. Eligible bookings go at random to treatment or holdout, 50/50 for a pilot. The draw is seeded, stored once and never redone.
  4. Draft. For treatment bookings only: message A, then at most one follow-up. Templates only (dry run), or a model API improves the wording. Local checks flag new numbers, banned phrases, promises and personal data.
  5. Approve and send. A person fills each [FILL], edits, approves, and sends from the booking platform.
  6. Report. Monthly in season: in-window changes and cancellations, treatment versus holdout, flexible and non-refundable separately, with 95% intervals and a "directional" label when the sample is small.

What the numbers say

    
    
      
  • Recommendation Use 50/50 for a pilot. The original brief held back 20%. An 80/20 split needs about 1.5 times as many bookings for the same answer: 6,212 instead of 4,072 at 15% to 12%. The rule of thumb, 1 / (4 x 0.8 x 0.2), says 1.56 times.
    • 
      
  • A single operator gets a directional read. With 1,500 bookings and the top 10% eligible, you see about 150 eligible bookings a season, 75 in each arm at 50/50. A confirmatory answer needs about 27 seasons. A directional read is still worth having; it is not evidence to scale on.
    • 
      
  • Pooling through ATIA can answer it in one season. About 28 operators running the same rules, the same messages and the same 50/50 split, sharing counts (never guest data), reach the sample size together. That needs a written data-sharing agreement Contractual
    • 
    

    
    
    
    
Method: normal approximation for two proportions, pooled rate under "no effect", rounded up to whole bookings. The 15% baseline is an example, not a measured Alaska figure: use your own last season. Estimates only.

How you will know it worked

    
Number 4 · In-window changes and cancellations, against the holdout

    
Bookings changed or cancelled inside the free-cancellation window, divided by bookings, counted monthly in season. Show flexible and non-refundable bookings separately. Compare treatment with holdout by assigned group, whether or not each draft was sent. Leave operator cancellations (a marine warning) out.

    
Revenue kept: the value of in-window cancellations in each group, per booking. Cost: staff minutes per approved draft, plus API fees. Guardrails: complaints and opt-outs.

Before you use it

    
AI drafts. A person approves. Every draft is marked DRAFT - PENDING OWNER REVIEW and waits in a queue. A person who knows the operation checks every fact, fills every [FILL] and sends approved messages from the booking platform. Local checks and AI self-review are not verification.

    
    
      
  • Recommendation Test on the fictional data first, then on a copy of your own export with personal columns removed. Never paste guest names, emails, phone numbers, card numbers, IDs or medical information into any tool.
    • 
      
  • Contractual Read your booking platform's API terms, your OTA agreements and your model provider's data terms before connecting anything.
    • 
      
  • Policy choice Your cancellation terms, any lock-in offer and the holdout share are business decisions. The AI words them; it does not set them.
    • 
      
  • Recommendation Safety-critical lines (weather go/no-go, sea state, glacier, wildlife) are owned by your captain or operations lead.
