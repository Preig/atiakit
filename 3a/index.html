The AI trip kit

From one booking, AI drafts a one-page plan for your guest: your trip anchored, partner options for open half-days, what to wear that month and a weather plan B. This page checks ages and time clashes before the AI sees anything, and checks the draft after. A person approves it before it goes out.

The pain it fixes

For the traveler

39% of US travelers name coordinating the different parts of a trip as a frustrating moment in planning.

In the diary study behind the same report, the handoffs between platforms were the most frequent source of negative sentiment. And planning does not stop at booking: 78% keep planning after they book, and 68% research restaurants and activities.

For your business

An Alaska trip is a flight, a car or train, a lodge, two tours and gear rental: several businesses, several handoffs. Each handoff brings repeat questions and missed add-ons.

A one-page kit puts your trip, two trusted partners and a weather plan B in one place. It answers questions once and sends guests to people you know.

My read This pain is probably larger in Alaska than the national 39%, because trips here involve several businesses by default. Nobody has measured it.

Source: Skift Research + McKinsey, Winning Hearts in an Age of Infinite Travel Choices, Sept 2026 · survey of 1,015 US travelers, data as of June 2026 (39%: Exhibit 6; 78% and 68%: p26) · diary study of 26 travelers and 211 sessions, July 2026, directional (handoffs: p18) · US travelers, not Alaska visitors. Figures last verified 2026-10-10.

How to use it

  1. Once: enter your approved facts (from 1.a), your partners and a month note. This browser remembers them. Nothing is sent anywhere.
  2. Per booking: dates, party size, children's ages, what they booked and when. No names, emails or phone numbers.
  3. Read the pre-checks. The page has already written them into the prompt as rules: who is too young for which partner, which departures clash, which facts are missing.
  4. Copy the prompt into Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini. A free tier works. Check the tool's data-use setting first.
  5. Paste the draft back into "Check the draft" and fix every flag.
  6. A person approves it, drops it into the kit template, saves it as a PDF and sends it with the confirmation.

Build your trip-kit prompt

The boxes start with the fictional teaching business, Bay Light Day Cruises in Seward, and its two fictional partners. Replace them with your own approved facts.

Your business (set up once)

Only July is filled in for the demo. Any other month becomes [FILL] in the prompt, and the AI is told not to describe weather for it. Write notes from a source you trust and have the owner approve them.

Your partners (set up once)

One block per partner. Leave a box empty when you do not know: the page writes [FILL] there and tells the AI not to guess. List a partner only after they have confirmed their facts with you.

This booking, de-identified

No guest names, emails, phone numbers, card numbers, ID numbers or medical details. Dates, ages and the product are enough.

      Before you use it
      
    
      AI drafts. You approve. Every draft carries DRAFT - PENDING OWNER REVIEW until a person has checked it against your fact sheet and partner list. Asking the AI to check its own work is not a check.
      
    
        
      
      • Legal mandate Guide licensing is yours and your partners' to hold, and it cannot be delegated. A partner who guides big game hunts holds a license under Alaska Statutes Title 8, Chapter 54 (AS 08.54); a sport-fishing guide and guide business hold a current ADF&G registration, renewed each year. Your kit cannot stand in for either, and an AI cannot check them. Verify them yourself before you list the partner.
        • 
      
      • Recommendation Weather, water, glacier and wildlife calls belong to your captain or another qualified person. The AI drafts the plan B text; that person approves it and makes the go or no-go call on the day. Do not promise weather or wildlife.
        • 
      
      • Recommendation Keep guest names, emails, phone numbers, card numbers, ID numbers and medical details out of AI tools. Ages, dates and the product are enough. Check your AI tool's data-use setting. This page's privacy check is a first pass only.
        • 
      
      • Contractual Check your referral agreement with each partner before you list them: who confirms their times and terms, how codes are counted, who answers guest questions about their trip. Recommendation If you have no written agreement yet, put one in writing first.
        • 
      
      • Contractual For guests who booked through Viator or another OTA, check your OTA supplier agreement before you send your own messages or partner offers outside the platform.
        • 
      
      • Legal mandate The T-30 message promotes an offer, so treat it as commercial email under CAN-SPAM (15 U.S.C. 7701 to 7713; FTC rule 16 CFR Part 316): include your postal address and a working way to opt out. Marketing texts sent through an automated texting platform need the guest's prior express written consent under the TCPA (47 U.S.C. 227; FCC rule 47 CFR 64.1200).
        • 
      
      • Legal mandate In the After message, offer nothing in exchange for a review that depends on what it says (FTC rule 16 CFR 465.4). Contractual Review sites add their own rules; Google's review policy, for example, bars asking only happy customers. Check each platform's policy.
        • 
      
      • Legal mandate Lodges: a message that shows a short-term lodging price shows the total price, mandatory fees included (FTC rule 16 CFR Part 464, in effect since May 12, 2025). The rule does not cover tours.
        • 
      
      • Policy choice Your price-drop stance, the gap between activities, and how many days after the trip you ask for a review.
        • 
      
      • Recommendation Nothing on this page sends, books, holds or changes anything. A person sends the kit and schedules the messages.
